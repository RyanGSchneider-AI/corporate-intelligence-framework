# Feature Backlog
> Part of the Corporate Intelligence Framework — Product
>
> The single prioritized list of features and directions the organization intends to pursue — the bridge between *deciding what is worth doing* (Business-Case, Strategy-and-Intent) and *delivering it* (Product-Build-Records).
>
> **This is the seam between the two levels of product work.** *Prioritization* happens here and in the Business Case: which directions earn investment, and in what relative order. *Delivery* happens in Product Build Records: durable teams pull from the top of this list and own how and when the work is sequenced and shipped. Work is not pushed onto teams, and it does not route back through a Business Case for re-approval — teams pull from this backlog by capacity.
>
> **Ordered by relative business value, not absolute cost.** Items are ranked against each other by the value they deliver relative to their size — the agile default — not by a precise dollar figure. A rough relative ranking that teams trust is worth more than a precise one nobody pulls from.
>
> **This document is always live.** It is continuously reordered as value, learning, and strategy change. Items enter as candidates, are promoted when prioritized, are pulled into delivery, and are retired when done or dropped.

## 1. How This Works

**Two levels, one seam.** The organization decides *which directions are worth pursuing* through Strategy-and-Intent and Business Cases. Those decisions land here as ranked items. Durable teams then **pull** from the top of the list as they have capacity and deliver through Product Build Records, owning the sequencing of the actual work. Prioritization sets order; the team owns delivery.

**Pull, not push.** Nobody assigns items down to a team, and a team does not return to the Business Case for per-effort approval. The team pulls the highest-value item it has capacity for and runs it end to end.

**Relative, not absolute.** Ranking is comparative — *this is worth more than that, for roughly this much effort* — not a precise cost model. Reach for ROM or Three-Point estimation (Cost-and-Benefit-Framework) only when a specific financial commitment needs a money figure; it is not required to rank the backlog.

**Method:** [e.g., Relative business value vs. relative size; reviewed at each planning rhythm]

> A common lightweight model is **WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)**: rank by *cost of delay ÷ job size*, where cost of delay combines business value, time-criticality, and risk/opportunity enablement. Use it if it helps; a simple high/medium/low value-vs-size judgment is also fine. The point is a shared, relative order — not a precise number.

## 4. How Teams Pull

- A team with capacity pulls the highest-ranked **Prioritized** item it can take on, sets its Status to **In Delivery**, and opens or continues a Product Build Record.
- The team owns sequencing of the work within and around that item — the backlog sets *what is most valuable next*, not *how to build it*.
- Findings from delivery flow back as context: an item may be re-ranked, split, or dropped based on what a team learns. Where a finding materially changes the economics, note it on the linked Business Case — as context, not re-approval.
- Completed items are marked **Done** and retired; dropped items keep a one-line reason so the decision is not relitigated later.
